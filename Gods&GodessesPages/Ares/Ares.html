ARES - THE GREEK GOD OF WAR

Facts

Ares is the Greek god of war and violence. He is one of the Olympian gods and is often depicted holding weapons like spears or hammers. Ares is known for his aggressive nature and love for battle. He is associated with bloodshed and the chaos aspect of war. Unlike other gods who are more benevolent, Ares is often portrayed as cruel and merciless.

Relationships

Primary lover: Aphrodite. Though he has taken other lovers as well. Children: Eros, Phobos, Deimos, Harmonia, Anteros, Himeros, Pothos. These are his children with Aphrodite, but he also fathered other children from different partners. Parents: Zeus and Hera. Siblings: Hephaestus, Athena, Eris, Hebe, and Enyo.

Beliefs and goals

Ares is driven by a desire for violent conflict, bloodlust, and physical courage, thriving on the raw chaos and brutality of warfare rather than strategic victory or civic order. He disregards order and diplomacy, often siding with factions who wish for brutal fights, fierce competition, and uncontrollable violence. He firmly believes victory belongs to the ones who fought the most brutal. Ares even instigates his own conflicts to satisfy his thirst for battle, participating in wars alongside Phobos, Deimos, and Eris. Overall, Ares hates peace and quiet for he embodies the chaos, suffering, and consequences of war. Despite that, he is unapologetically loyal to his people and will avenge them if anyone dares to offend.

Stories & important events

The First Murder Trial in Athens.

After Poseidon's son, Halirrhothius, assaulted Ares's daughter, Alcippe, Ares killed him. Poseidon brought charges against Ares, leading to the first murder trial on a hill in Athens. The gods deemed Ares not guilty and let him go.

The Sisyphus Incident

When King Sisyphus cheated death by chaining up Thanatos (the god of death), mortals could no longer die. Ares intervened because, to him, wars had become pointless without casualties. Ares freed Thanatos and turned Sisyphus over to him, restoring mortality to the battlefield.

Ares vs. Athena

In the Trojan war, Ares and Athena backed opposing sides. Ares fought for Troy, while Athena supported the Greeks. In the Iliad, Athena guided the mortal, Diomedes's, spear to wound Ares, and later knocked Ares unconscious with a massive boulder when he attacked her directly.

Trivia

Ares' Roman equivalent is Mars. The two moons of planet Mars are named after Ares' twin sons, Phobos and Deimos. In Roman languages, Tuesday is named after him (Dies Martis). Despite his violent nature, Ares is the patron god of protecting women. He also loves his children deeply. His brother, Hephaestus, is married to Ares' lover, Aphrodite, due to Zeus ordering the marriage.
