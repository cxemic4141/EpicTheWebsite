APOLLO

THE GOD OF THE SUN, MUSIC & PROPHECY

⋆⊱𖤓⊰⋆

Facts

Apollo is the Greek God of the sun, music, prophecy, healing, archery and light. He`s intelligent, artistic, charming, and can become quite prideful due to his competitiveness. With his ability to see the future, he shared his knowledge to mortals through prophets and oracles, most famously speaking through the Oracle of Delphi, which is a prophetic shrine that advised rulers, lawmakers, and citizens on war. He is also known for his musical skills, playing the lyre and leading the Muses.

Beliefs and Goals

Apollo valued knowledge, artistic expression, music and harmony. He believed that prophecies and divine truth were important as they were a means to understand the will of the gods and guide mortal life. He particulary took pride in his abilities, particularly music, archery, and prophecy.

Stories & Important Events

Birth

Apollo was born on the island of Delos, which was a sacred place for his mother Leto. His birth was not without challenges, as Hera, the wife of Zeus, was jealous of Leto and forbade her from giving birth on any land that saw the light of day. However, Delos was a floating island and thus not considered land, allowing Leto to give birth to Apollo and his twin sister Artemis. Apollo's birth was celebrated with great joy and he quickly became known for his beauty and divine attributes.

Daphne

Apollo fell in love with Daphne after being struck by one of Ero's golden arrows. Daphne was a naiad who was devoted to the goddess Artemis and wished to remain chaste. When Apollo told her his feelings she rejected him, yet that did not stop him and instead he chased her. Out of desperation, Daphne prayed to her father, the river god Peneus, to help her escape. In response, Peneus transformed Daphne into a laurel tree just as Apollo was about to catch her. Heartbroken, Apollo honored Daphne by making the laurel tree sacred to him and wearing its leaves as a symbol of honor.

The God of Medicine

Coronis was a mortal woman who became the wife of Apollo. She was known for her beauty and wisdom, and was often depicted as a healer. However, she was eventually killed by Apollo in a fit of rage after discovering her infidelity when a white raven told him of the betrayal. Yet, right after, he felt deep regret and in anger he turned the raven's feathers black. When Coronis was killed she was pregnant, and Apollo cut the unborn child, Asclepius, from her womb. Asclepius would go on to become the god of medicine and healing.

Trivia

  • He won a musical contest against the satyr Marsyas, and as punishment for losing he tied him to a tree and skinned him alive.
  • Although commonly associated with the sun, Helios was originally the personification of the Sun in Greek mythology.
  • He sent a plague during the Trojan War as greeks dishonored his priest, Chryses.
  • After Zeus struck down his son, Asclepius for ressurecting the dead, Apollo killed the Cyclopes who forged Zeus's thunderbolts, which lead to his temporary exile as a mortal servant.
