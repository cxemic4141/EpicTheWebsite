Facts

Aphrodite is the Greek goddess of love, beauty, desires and procreation. It is said that her beauty is overwhelmingly divine, that it captivates onlookers, inspire devotion, and can ignite rilvary or conflict over her. She can also spark passion, attraction, and infatuation in mortals and god alike. If she favors you, she will occassionally shield you in battle, just like how she protected favored lovers and couples such as Aeneas and Paris. In regions like Sparta, she was worshipped with warrior attributes.

Beliefs and Goals

As the Goddess of Beauty, she believes that mere love and beauty can have tremendous influence over gods and mortals. After all, even Zeus couldn't resist. Despite using her powers to manipulate attraction and infatuation, she deeply values affection and admiration, and enjoys inspiring romantic relationships. Her jealousy for mortal beauty would often lead to her cursing her rivals and anyone who dared threaten her beauty would be cursed to desire their own fathers or even young lovers.

Stories & Important Events

Birth

In some versions, Aphrodite was born from seafoam, that was created after Cronus castrated his father, Uranus, and threw his sevred genitals into the ocean. In other versions she was the daughter of Zeus and the goddess Dione. She was also celebrated during festivals dedicated to her worship, such as the Aphrodisia.

The Fairest of Them All

Paris was a Trojan prince, who was asked to choose the fairest goddess between Hera, Athena and Aphrodite. Hera offered Paris kingship, wealth, and rule over lands. Athena offered him wisdom, skill and victory in war, and the glory of becoming a great warrior. Aphrodite promised him the most beautiful woman in the world, Helen of Sparta. With careful thinking and a long thoughtful process, Paris ultimately decided.. to take Aphrodite's offer. This event helped lead to the Trojan War.

Cat's Out of The Bag.. or rather net?

Hephaestus and Aphrodite's marriage was not by choice. It was arranged by other gods to stop rivals gods from figting over Aphrodite, because uncontrolled rivalry among the Olympians risked a civil war that could destroy cosmic order. This arrangement caused extreme displeasure to Ares and Aphrodite, who were already in love. The two did not wish to give up the connection they had, so they decided to continue their relationship in secret. Eventuallly Hephaestus found out about the affair and instead of confronting Ares, he made an invisble net that could be sprung up anytime he chose. When the time came he caught Ares and Aphrodite in the middle of the act and trapped the two lovers in a shameful position that they couldn't free themselves from. Hephaestus then invited all the gods and goddesses to see the two lovers and humiliate them. The goddesses declined out of respect, but many gods came mainly to see Aphrodite's beauty and shamed Ares. The two lovers stayed in that position for days until Hepaestus was satisfied.

Trivia

  • It is said that only three virgin goddesses: Athena, Artemis, and Hestia were immune to her powers.
  • Her sacred animal is the dove.
  • Her roman counterpart is Venus.
  • She is associated with the famous sculpture "Venus de Milo" and the painting "The Birth of Venus".
  • Despite being the goddess of love, she rarely had a lasting, peaceful love of her own.
